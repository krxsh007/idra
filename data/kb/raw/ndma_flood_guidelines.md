# National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) Guidelines: Management of Urban Floods

**Document Reference:** NDMA-SOP-UF-2023  
**Publication Date:** 2023-05-15  
**Author:** National Disaster Management Authority, Government of India  
**Applicability:** National & State Emergency Operations Centers, Incident Commanders  

---

## 1. Introduction and Objectives
Urban flooding is significantly different from rural flooding. Urbanization leads to developed catchments which increase the flood peaks by 1.8 to 8 times and flood volumes by up to 6 times. As a result, flood runoff occurs very quickly, with minimal time for warning.

The objective of these guidelines is to establish standardized operating procedures for:
1. Multi-agency coordination during intense urban precipitation events.
2. Rapid staging and deployment of National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) units.
3. Safe evacuation routing prioritizing vulnerable demographics (elderly, infants, hospitalized patients).

---

## 2. Early Warning & Thresholds
Urban flood risk levels are categorized into four operational states:

1. **State Green (Normal):** Rainfall accumulation < 30mm in 24 hours. Routine drainage monitoring.
2. **State Yellow (Watch):** Rainfall accumulation 30mm–70mm in 24 hours or localized waterlogging > 0.3 meters. Pre-positioning of inflatable rescue boats.
3. **State Orange (Alert):** Rainfall accumulation 70mm–150mm in 24 hours or inundation depth > 0.6 meters on arterial roads. Activation of Incident Command System (ICS).
4. **State Red (Emergency):** Rainfall accumulation > 150mm in 24 hours or breach of arterial canals/water bodies. Full emergency mobilization and mandatory evacuation of low-lying settlements.

---

## 3. Search and Rescue (SAR) Protocol
- Rescue boats must operate at minimum teams of 4 personnel (1 coxswain, 1 navigator, 2 rescue swimmers).
- All personnel operating within floodwaters exceeding 0.5m depth must wear Type III/V Personal Flotation Devices (PFDs) and high-visibility reflective helmets.
- Rescue teams must avoid underwater electrical hazards by coordinating immediate power substation de-energization with the state electricity board prior to operating inflatable motorized boats.
- Priority of rescue:
  1. Bedridden and critical medical patients in ground-floor hospital wards.
  2. Isolated pregnant women, infants, and unaccompanied minors.
  3. Trapped citizens in single-story structures with rising water levels.

---

## 4. Emergency Relief Shelter Management
- All designated relief shelters must be located outside the 100-year flood inundation zone or situated on elevated ground / upper floors.
- Power redundancy: Every shelter must maintain an operational diesel generator with minimum 72-hour fuel reserve.
- Potable water supply: Minimum 15 liters per person per day must be available on-site through reverse osmosis tankers or chlorination distribution.
- Sanitation facilities: Minimum 1 latrine per 20 persons, segregated by gender with adequate hygiene facilities.
